## Abstract

Vivid Reports are about understanding social, biological, medical, sporting and technological systems, about the models we build of them, and about how those models relate to the words we use to describe them. We publish three types of resources:

1. Applications which combine models and words to produce insight.
2. Methodological and theoretical contributions where models and words are combined.
3. Description of Agentic AI solutions, where human decision-making is placed in the centre.

The Vivid Reports series is itself an AI agent. Submissions are written together with an agent, reviews will be semi-automated and the repository itself can provide help and support to application builders and researchers.

## What we aim to publish

Vivid Reports is a forum for developing ways of exploiting the capabilities of agentic AI in data science and research. By the end of 2024, an estimated 29% of the Python functions contributed to open-source projects in the United States were generated by AI [@daniotti2025].  Programmers who used AI more often tried new libraries and new combinations of them, which the authors read as a move into areas of programming new to them. 

A profound change brought about by Agentic AI lies in how it produces a new relationship between words and models. Until recently, a model reached the people who used it through a statistician or data scientist, who knew which set of equations answered which question, what the mathematics assumed and when it should not be trusted. Language models can now do part (although not all) of that work themselves [@majumder2025; @chen2025; @gu2024blade]. 

Many models of the social world are stated in words, with their assumptions left informal rather than written as equations or code [@guest2021; @robinaugh2021]. Language models can now carry such knowledge into new settings. For example, GPT-4 predicted the results of 70 survey experiments about as well as human forecasters, including studies published after its training data [@ashokkumar2026], and language-model agents can state a hypothesis as a causal model and test it in simulation [@manning2024]. These results blur the lines between what can be said mathematically or statistically and what can be said in human language.

Vivid Insights publishes reports which recognise these changes and looks to shape future approaches to modelling and data science on the basis of them.

We publish three types of report. The first type of article applies models and words to give people insight to act on. In the past year, applications that join language and statistical models have started to appear. For example, a multi-agent system has provided suggestions of how existing drugs might be used for new treatments [@ghareeb2026]  and studies have shown how research tasks can be carried out by agents throughout biomedicine [@huang2026]. Social systems have their own examples. An AI mediator that wrote group statements from participants' own opinions helped people on opposing sides of political issues find common ground [@tessler2024]. Personalised, evidence-based conversations with a language model reduced belief in conspiracy theories by about a fifth, and the effect lasted at least two months [@costello2024]. In the second Vivid Report, a conversational analyst is developed which chooses which model answers a footballing question and explains what the answer means [@sumpter2026earpiece]. Among the first reports in this series, report 0007 describes an agentic mentor that supports African women entering technology careers, report 0009 a workshop on how generative AI might help caseworkers assess asylum claims, and report 0011 an explainable model of electrocardiograms.

The second kind of article is methodological or theoretical, focusing on relating models and words. Broadly speaking, there are at least three ways of getting from a model to words: build a model that can be read directly, a so called interpretable model [@rudin2019]; narrate the output of an explanation of a machine learning model [@zytek2024]; or reverse-engineer the computations inside a neural network into human concepts [@bereska2024]. We welcome all three approaches. The first article in this report series gives an example of the first of these: showing how to turn a statistical model of the data into a rule-based description, deciding what is said and in what order, and a language model then writes only that description out as fluent text [@caut2025]. Often the key Report 0005 sets out a method for writing storyboards that explain how a chatbot will behave before it is built, and report 0010 a framework for communicating an individual's health risk from population models in words.

A third kind of article describes methods for building agentic AI systems that work with humans. @schluntz2024 distinguish workflows, in which language models and tools follow predefined code paths, from agents, in which the model directs its own process and use of tools. Built well, such systems help the people who make the decisions. With an agent that drew on treatment guidelines and earlier tumour-board cases, junior doctors came close to the treatment choices of senior specialists [@zoller2026]. A language model fine-tuned on more than 160 psychology experiments predicted the choices of new participants better than existing cognitive models [@binz2025].  Co-Scientist generates and refines hypotheses from a scientist's own research goals, and drug candidates it proposed for acute myeloid leukaemia were confirmed in laboratory tests on cells [@gottweis2026]. Vivid Reports are built on that approach: the report repository is itself an agent that can explain the concepts written about in the reports themselves. Report 0004 describes building a football application with a coding agent, report 0006 a course in which statisticians and data scientists stay in control of an agent doing their analysis, and report 0008 specialised agents that guide a user through choosing a machine learning model one step at a time.

Language models already take part in writing and reviewing science. An estimated 6.5 to 16.9 percent of peer-review text at four AI conferences in 2023 and 2024 was substantially modified by a language model, and the share of model-modified text in published papers is rising steadily, mostly in computer science [@liang2024mapping]. However, a difficulty in the agentic approach is keeping the human in the loop. @mitchell2026 argue that current agent designs make oversight hard, and that relying on agents erodes the skills that oversight depends on. Among knowledge workers, higher confidence in generative AI was associated with less critical thinking [@lee2025], and in a small study of essay writing, people who used a language model had the lowest sense of ownership of their essays and struggled to quote from them [@kosmyna2025]. Vivid Insights encourages a way of working with agents that addresses these issues, in which the author makes the decisions, do the thinking first, and read everything the agent writes. 

We encourage incremental contributions. Given the agentic nature of the series, we do not gatekeep based on some preconceived notion of novelty. ## Writing your report with the Vivid Insights agent

Each Vivid Report is written in a shared repository, in one folder that holds the report, its references, its code and the material it rests on. The author works with an AI coding agent, and the article is a single Markdown file that both of them edit and that is built into the published page. Every change is committed, so the history of the article, including which sentences the agent wrote and which the author changed, can be read back.

Writing follows six phases: sketch the idea, draft the abstract, revise, write the start and end together, revise the abstract, and simplify and revise. At the first step, the agent gathers the material the article will rest on, such as code, saved model outputs, earlier papers and notes, and records where each came from. In the second step, author and agent agree the three points the article makes and put them into a working abstract. The abstract is then the template that each later section is checked against: the Introduction gives one paragraph of background to each point, the Methods and Results take them in the same order, and the Discussion answers them.

The agent is instructed to keep the author's judgement at the centre. The author decides what the article says, and is asked to go first: to name the point of a paragraph before it is drafted, the papers they know before a search, and what they expected before a result is written up. The agent writes its drafts directly into the article, where the author edits them, and reads the author's edits before its next change. It works one step at a time and stops, so that each step can be read before the next begins.

The agent checks the writing as it goes. Every quoted model output is taken from its saved file, and the build checks it word for word. Every cited work is one the agent has opened, and when a paper cannot be opened the author is asked to download it. Every number comes from a saved output. When the author corrects how the agent writes or works, the correction is added, with the sentence that prompted it, to a set of writing rules that every later article follows. This article was written that way, and its history is in its repository.
